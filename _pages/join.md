CIS Lab is looking for students who want to find out how deployed security systems actually behave. If you have ever wondered whether the padlock in your browser means what you were told it means, we would like to hear from you.

---

## What you would work on

Most projects follow a similar shape. You pick a mechanism that is widely deployed and widely trusted, build a tool that measures it across a real ecosystem rather than a few examples, and then work out what the measurements mean. Current directions are described on the [research]({{ '/research/' | relative_url }}) page: the TLS and PKI ecosystem, software supply chain security, and encrypted data search.

This is hands-on work. You will write code, read specifications carefully, and spend real time on the gap between the two.

---

## What we look for

None of these is a hard requirement, and nobody arrives with all of them.

- Comfort writing code you will later have to debug. Python and C are the common ones here; the language matters less than the willingness to read your own output critically.
- Enough background to read a networking or systems paper and get the gist. Courses in computer networks, operating systems, or information security help.
- Patience with results that do not come out clean the first time. Measurement work is mostly this.
- Enough English to read papers and write them. We publish at international venues.

Undergraduates are welcome. Several students have started with us before graduating and continued into the graduate program.

---

## How to apply

Email <a href="mailto:hskwon@inha.ac.kr">hskwon@inha.ac.kr</a>. Please include:

1. **A CV or transcript.** Whatever you have is fine.
2. **What caught your attention.** Name a paper, a topic on the research page, or a security question you have been curious about, and say a sentence or two about why. This matters more than your grades.
3. **When you would start,** and whether you are applying for a graduate program, an undergraduate research position, or an internship.

Put `[CIS Lab]` at the start of the subject line so the mail does not get lost.

Feel free to drop by Room #1110, Inha Hi-Tech Center, but email first so we can make sure someone is there.
